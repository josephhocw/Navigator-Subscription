When you open the order panel for the first time, it can look busy. It needn't. There are only three order types, and they answer one question: when do you want to get in? Once that clicks, the buttons stop being intimidating.

## The three order types

- **Market** — buy or sell now, at the current price. You press the button and you're in.
- **Limit** — buy or sell only at your price or better. You set a price and wait for the market to come to you.
- **Stop** — your trade kicks in once the price passes a level you've set. People use it to catch a move that's breaking out, or as a protective exit if the price turns against them.

<GuideOrderTypes />

## When to use each

Here's the short version, side by side.

| Order type | What it does | When to use it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Market | Gets you in straightaway at the current price | You want to trade now and the exact price isn't critical |
| Limit | Waits to buy lower, or sell higher, than the price right now | You'd rather hold out for a better price than chase it |
| Stop | Triggers only after the price moves past a level you set | You want to join a breakout, or have a price set to close you out if it turns |

Limit and stop both park an instruction and wait. The difference is the direction the price has to move to set them off: a limit buy waits for a dip, a stop buy waits for a push higher.

One thing to keep straight: the **stop** here is an *entry* order, the way you get into a trade. It's not the same as a **stop-loss**, which is a protective exit that closes a trade you're already in. You'll set stop-losses, and take-profits, on the next page when you place a trade.

<GuideCallout title="Those buttons look complicated">
They aren't, once you've read the three above. And for your very first trade you don't need limit or stop at all.

**Market is all you need.** It does one thing: get you in at the current price. You can start there, place a small trade, and add the others to your toolkit later.
</GuideCallout>
