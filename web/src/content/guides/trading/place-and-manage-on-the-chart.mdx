This is the one that matters: your first trade, from open to close, right on the chart. The same steps work for everyone. Take it slowly, start small, and you'll have nothing to undo.

<GuideChecks title="What you'll need">
- A funded trading account connected to TradingView (See [the connect step](/guides/connect-pepperstone-tradingview#connect-to-pepperstone))
- Funds in your account (See [the funding step](/guides/open-pepperstone-free-tradingview#add-funds) on how to deposit)
</GuideChecks>

## On your computer

- **Check you're connected.** Your account name and balance show at the bottom of the chart. If they're not there, go back to [the connect step](/guides/connect-pepperstone-tradingview) before you carry on.

<GuideShot src="/guides/step-6/account-connected.png" caption="Your connected account name and balance show in the panel at the bottom of the chart." />

- **Make your search tradable.** Under Symbol Search, click the white broker icon. From now on, every instrument you search for comes up as a chart you can trade on.

<GuideShot narrow src="/guides/step-6/symbol-search-pepperstone.png" caption="In Symbol Search, click the white broker icon." />

<GuideShot narrow src="/guides/step-6/pepperstone-only-results.png" caption="Now every result is a chart you can trade." />

- **Open the order panel.** Click Buy or Sell and the panel opens on the right. At the top you'll see Market, Limit and Stop. For your first trade, choose **Market** — it gets you in at the current price, no waiting.

<GuideShot narrow src="/guides/step-6/order-types.png" caption="The order-type tabs at the top of the panel. Market is the one you want for a first trade." />

- **Set your size.** Start with the minimum lot. Not sure what that is? Clear the lot-size box and the panel shows you the minimum. Keep an eye on this on some markets, Gold trades in lots of 100, so the size means more than it looks.
- **Set your protection before you place.** In the same panel, set a take-profit (where the trade closes in profit) and a stop-loss (where it closes if the price turns against you). Decide both before you click and your downside is set in advance. One honest note: in a fast or gapping market a stop-loss can close a little past your level, so it's a strong safety net, not a cast-iron guarantee.
- **Place it.** Press the Buy or Sell button to confirm. Your open trade then shows in the positions panel at the bottom of the chart.

<GuideShot narrow src="/guides/step-6/trading-panel.png" caption="The order panel — order type, lot size, and your take-profit and stop-loss, all in one place." />

- **Manage it from the chart.** Once a trade is open you don't have to leave the chart to look after it. To change your take-profit or stop-loss, drag its line on the chart or edit the figure in the positions panel. To close early, click the close button on the position. You can do this any time the market is open. (If TradingView itself ever has a problem, you can manage the same trade from [your trading terminal](/guides/trading/your-trading-terminal) instead.)

## On your phone

- The flow is the same in the app. Open the chart and check your account is connected at the bottom.
- Open the order panel: on iPhone tap the **Buy/Sell** buttons, on Android tap the **two-arrows** symbol.
- Choose **Market**, set the minimum size, and set your take-profit and stop-loss in the same panel before you place.
- Your open trade shows in the positions panel, and you can modify or close it there too.

## Two things the chart won't show you

The chart is built for trading, not for digging up detail. Two things it can't do — and both live on your trading terminal:

- **The full instrument information** — leverage, swap and commission — *before* you place a trade. The chart shows you the lot size, not the rest.
- **More than a day or two of history.** For your complete record of past trades, you go to the broker.

That, plus a couple of extra tools and your backup way in, is what's next: [your trading terminal](/guides/trading/your-trading-terminal).

<GuideCallout title="What if I mess it up, or lose money on my first trade?">
The downside is in your hands, set before you click.

Start at the minimum lot size, so any price move stays small, and set your stop-loss before you place the order, so the trade closes itself if it turns against you. Do those two and you've capped the worst case before the trade even opens.
</GuideCallout>
