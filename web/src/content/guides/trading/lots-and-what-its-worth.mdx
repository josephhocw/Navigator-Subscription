On the last page, a 100-point move on the Nasdaq 100 came to about US$100. This page shows the simple rule behind that figure, so your profit and loss stops feeling like a black box. We're only covering the mechanics here, not how much you should put on.

## What a lot is

- A **lot** is the bundle you trade in. You choose how many lots to buy or sell.
- The **lot size** is how many units of the instrument are packed into one lot. It's a figure your platform shows you, in the order panel under "Order info".

<GuideShot narrow maxHeight="400px" src="/guides/trading/lots/lot-size-panel.png" caption="On the order panel, 'Lot size' tells you how many units are in one lot. Here, one lot of the Japan 225 is 100." />

One lot is a different number of units on each market. As a rough guide:

| Market | Lot size of one standard lot |
| --- | --- |
| Shares | 1 (a single share) |
| Forex | 100,000 units of the currency |
| Gold | 100 ounces |
| Indices | set per index, and shown on your panel (the Japan 225 above is 100) |

## What each move is worth

- The simplest way to picture it: how many **units** are you holding? Your number of lots, times the lot size, is your total.
- Each unit moves dollar-for-dollar with the price. Hold **1 unit** and a $1 move is worth $1 to you. Hold **100 units** and that same $1 move is worth $100.
- So your result is just **your total units, times how far the price moves.**
- On the Nasdaq 100, one lot behaves like a single unit, so it's about a dollar for every point: a 100-point move is about US$100. On gold, one lot is 100 units (100 ounces), so a US$1 move an ounce is about US$100. Same rule, different lot size.

## You can start tiny

- You don't have to trade a full lot. A smaller lot holds fewer units, so it scales the result straight down.
- A 0.1 lot holds a tenth of the units, so it's a tenth of the result.
- Pick a small lot to begin with and the numbers stay small while you find your feet.

<GuideCallout title="Not sure what the smallest size is?">
You can't leave the Lots box empty, but type a **0** in it (or any size below the minimum) and the platform tells you the smallest size you're allowed to trade.
</GuideCallout>
