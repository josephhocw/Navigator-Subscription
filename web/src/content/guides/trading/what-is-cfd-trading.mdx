Before you place a trade, it helps to know exactly what you're trading. The Navigator works on CFDs. The name sounds technical, but the idea behind it is simple.

## What a CFD actually is

- CFD stands for "contract for difference". You trade the change in a price, not the thing itself.
- Trade a CFD on the Nasdaq 100 and you own no Nasdaq shares. You settle the difference between the price when you open the trade and when you close it.
- If it moves your way, that difference is your profit. If it moves against you, it's your loss.
- One way to picture it: you and a friend agree on today's gold price, and later whoever called the direction right collects the difference. Neither of you ever bought any gold.

## Buy or sell, you can profit either way

- **Buy**, or go long, if you think the price will rise. You profit as it goes up.
- **Sell**, or go short, if you think the price will fall. You profit as it goes down.
- Selling to profit from a fall is the new part for most people. With ordinary shares you'd have to own them before you could sell; with a CFD you don't.

## Where a CFD's price comes from

- A CFD's price tracks the real market. Your broker quotes it at essentially the same price as the actual Nasdaq 100, gold, or share you'd see anywhere.
- So a Nasdaq 100 CFD follows the same ups and downs as the real Nasdaq 100. You're trading genuine market movement, not a number someone made up.
- The only small gaps are the trading costs built in, mainly the spread, plus a little adjustment if you hold overnight. We'll cover those on the fees page.

## Benefits of trading with CFDs

- **Profit either way** — go long or short, with no shares to borrow.
- **Many markets, one account** — indices, shares, forex, metals, crypto.
- **Nothing to own or store** — no settlement and no taking delivery; you only trade the price.
- **Less cash tied up** — you put up a fraction of the trade's value (this is leverage).
- **Low costs** — tight spreads on the major markets.

And the trade-offs, so you go in clear-eyed:

- **Leverage cuts both ways** — it can magnify losses too, and a position can be closed out against you. More on the leverage page.
- **Overnight fees** — a small cost to hold past the day, so not for keeping for years.
- **No ownership** — not the route for long-term investing in a company.
