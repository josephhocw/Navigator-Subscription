"Hidden fees" is the worry most people bring to trading. The good news is there's nothing hidden here. A CFD trade has only three costs, and once you've seen them you can stop wondering what's eating into your money.

## The three costs

- **Spread** — the main cost, and the only one on most trades. It's the small gap between the price you can sell at and the price you can buy at.
- **Commission** — a small fee that only applies to forex and share trades. On the markets most people start with, like the indices, there's no commission at all.
- **Swap** — a small charge, or sometimes a small credit, for holding a trade overnight. If you open and close on the same day, it never applies.

| Cost | When it applies | Rough size |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Spread | Every trade | Tiny on the major markets |
| Commission | Forex and shares only | A few dollars; none on indices |
| Swap | Only if held overnight | Small, can be a charge or a credit |

That's the whole list. No account fee, no monthly fee, no charge to place or close a trade beyond these.

## The spread, the cost that's always there

- The spread is the difference between the buy price and the sell price at any moment.
- You don't pay it as a separate line. It's already built into the two prices you see, so you cross it the instant you open a trade.
- This is where the broker matters. **Pepperstone's spreads are very competitive, especially on the indices** like the Nasdaq 100, which is one reason we pair the Navigator with it. A tighter spread means less cost to you on every trade.

## Commission, only on some markets

- Commission is a separate small fee, and it only shows up on **forex and share trades**. The indices have no commission, just the spread.
- As a rough guide: US shares run about 0.02 US dollars a share each way; Hong Kong shares about 0.15% each way; and forex on a standard account about 7 US dollars in total for a one-lot round trip. Treat those as illustrative figures to give you a sense of scale.
- So if you start on an index, commission simply doesn't enter the picture.

## Swap, only if you hold overnight

- If you keep a trade open past the end of the trading day, there's a small overnight adjustment called the swap.
- It's there for a simple reason: holding a leveraged trade overnight means the broker is funding the borrowed part of it for you, so there's a small charge to carry it (and occasionally a credit the other way).
- It can go either way. Some days it's a small charge, and on some trades it's actually a small credit to you, depending on the market and interest rates.
- Close your trade the same day and you'll never see it. It's only a consideration if you hold a position for a while, which is why CFDs suit shorter-term trades rather than holding for years.

## Should I open my account in SGD or USD?

This one comes up a lot, so it's worth clearing up before you open your account. The short answer: **keep it in Singapore dollars. You don't need a US dollar account.**

Here's where the worry comes from. If you buy an actual US share through a broker like Moomoo, your Singapore dollars get converted into US dollars first, before you can buy anything, and the platform usually takes about 1% on that exchange. People feel that cost, so they think about holding US dollars to avoid it.

A CFD works differently:

- Your account balance is **not** converted to buy the trade. You trade directly in Singapore dollars, even on a market priced in US dollars.
- Only your profit or loss is worked out in Singapore dollars, at the live exchange rate of the moment, when you close.
- So there's no 1% conversion happening every time you buy or sell. Your balance just stays as it is.

**Bottom line: keep your account in Singapore dollars.** Opening a US dollar account to dodge a conversion fee solves a problem you don't have with CFDs.
