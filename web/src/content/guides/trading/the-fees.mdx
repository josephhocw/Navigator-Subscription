"Hidden fees" is the worry most people bring to trading. There's nothing hidden here. The fees you might expect — for holding an account or moving money — you simply don't pay. The only costs are three small trading fees, and we'll walk through each one.

- No account fee.
- No inactivity fee.
- No monthly fee.

## The three trading fees

These are the only costs that apply when you trade:

- **Spread** — the main cost, and the only one on most trades. It's the small gap between the price you can sell at and the price you can buy at.
- **Commission** — a small fee that **only applies to forex and share trades.** Zero commission on indices and commodities.
- **Swap** — a small charge, or sometimes a small credit, for holding a trade overnight. If you open and close on the same day, it never applies.

## The spread, the cost that's always there

- The spread is the difference between the buy price and the sell price at any moment.
- You don't pay it as a separate line. It's already built into the two prices you see, so you cross it the instant you open a trade.
- This is where the broker matters. **Pepperstone's spreads are very competitive, especially on the indices** like the Nasdaq 100, which is one reason we pair the Navigator with it. A tighter spread means less cost to you on every trade.

## Commission, only on some markets

Commission is a separate small fee that only applies to a few markets. Indices and commodities have none at all, so if you start on an index, commission simply doesn't enter the picture.

| Market | Commission per side |
| --- | --- |
| Indices | None |
| Commodities (e.g. gold) | None |
| Forex | US$3.50 |
| US shares and ETFs | US$0.02 a share |
| Hong Kong shares | 0.15% |
| Shares in other countries | 0.07%–0.10% |

## Swap, only if you hold overnight

**Swap** (also called the **overnight fee** or **rollover**) is a small adjustment for holding a trade overnight, meaning past the **daily cut-off** around **5am Singapore time**. Open and close on the same day and there's no swap at all.

It exists because holding a leveraged trade overnight means the broker funds the borrowed part for you, so a small amount changes hands each night to carry it.

### It can cost you, and sometimes it pays you

Swap can go either way, depending on the market and interest rates, and your **trade direction** usually decides which:

- A **long** trade (a buy) usually pays a small charge each night.
- A **short** trade (a sell) is usually much smaller, and **often a credit**, so you earn a little for holding.

To tell in advance, look up the instrument's swap: **a positive number is a credit** (paid to you), **a negative number is a charge** (you pay).

<p><u><strong>Example: Apple is priced at US$100 and you hold 100 shares</strong></u></p>

- Your position is US$100 × 100 shares = **US$10,000**.
- **Long** (rate −0.01697%): 10,000 × 0.01697% = about **US$1.70 charged** for each night you hold.
- **Short** (rate +0.00308%): 10,000 × 0.00308% = about **US$0.31 credited** for each night you hold.

Same trade, opposite direction: holding the long costs you about US$1.70 a night, while holding the short pays you about US$0.31.

### The weekend "three-day" swap

Most nights you hold, you pay or earn **one night** of swap. But the market behind your trade is closed on Saturday and Sunday, and that funding isn't skipped. It's collected in advance on one weekday.

So on that day you'll see about **three nights' swap at once**: the normal night plus the two weekend nights. Not an extra penalty, just the same nightly amount bundled.
