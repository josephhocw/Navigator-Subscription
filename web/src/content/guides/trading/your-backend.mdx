You place and manage your trades on the chart in TradingView, and that's all most people ever need. But sitting behind it is the broker platform itself, and it's worth knowing your way around it for one reason above all the others.

If TradingView ever has a problem, you log in to your backend and manage your open trades from there. It's your backup. A second, separate way in to the same live positions, so a glitch on one screen can never lock you out of your own trades.

<GuideBackendBackup />

So the backend is your safety net first. Beyond that, it's also the home for a few things the chart simply can't do: looking up an instrument's [leverage and fees](/guides/trading/instrument-information), setting a trailing stop-loss, and seeing your full trade history. We'll come to those.

<GuidePlatformCheck />

## On Pepperstone

Your backend here is **Pepperstone Webtrader**, which you open in a web browser. You sign in with the same details you use for Pepperstone.

- **Open it.** Log in to your Pepperstone account, then open Webtrader. You'll see your chart, your account balance, and a panel along the bottom for your trades.
- **Find your trades.** The bottom panel has tabs for **Positions** (trades open right now), **Orders** (trades waiting to trigger), and **Trade History** (everything you've already closed).
- **Manage a trade.** Right-click any open position to **close** it, or to **modify** it and change your take-profit or stop-loss. This is the backup for whatever you'd normally do on the chart.

<GuideShot src="/guides/trading/your-backend/pepperstone-webtrader.png" caption="Pepperstone Webtrader with the bottom panel showing the Positions, Orders, and Trade History tabs, and the right-click menu open on a position with Close and Modify." />

- **Your full record.** The **Trade History** tab is where your complete past trades live, going back further than the chart shows. It's the place to look when you want to review how you've done.

## On cTrader

Your backend here is **cTrader**. One thing trips people up at first: cTrader has its **own password**, separate from your usual login.

- **Set your password.** The first time, use the **Forgot Password** link on the cTrader login screen to set it. After that you log in normally.
- **Find your trades.** Everything is in the **Trade Watch** panel along the bottom. Its tabs are **Positions** (open now), **Orders** (waiting to trigger), **History** (closed trades), **Transactions** (money in and out), and **Balance**.
- **Manage a trade.** From Positions you can **modify** an open trade to change its take-profit or stop-loss, or **reverse** it (close it and open the opposite direction in one step).

<GuideShot src="/guides/trading/your-backend/ctrader-tradewatch.png" caption="The cTrader Trade Watch panel along the bottom, showing the Positions, Orders, History, Transactions, and Balance tabs, with an open position selected." />

### The extra protection cTrader gives you

cTrader can guard a trade in ways the chart can't:

- **Trailing stop-loss** — a stop-loss that follows the price as your trade moves into profit, locking in gains as it climbs instead of staying put.
- **Advanced Protection** — set several take-profit levels for one trade so you can bank profit in stages, and have your stop-loss move automatically to your entry price once the trade is ahead, so a winning trade can't turn into a loss.

<GuideShot src="/guides/trading/your-backend/ctrader-advanced-protection.png" caption="The cTrader Advanced Protection settings on a position: multiple take-profit levels and the automatic stop-to-breakeven option." />

<GuideCallout title="One thing to know about these cTrader tools">
The trailing stop-loss and Advanced Protection only run while **cTrader is open and online** on your device. Close the app, lose your connection, or shut down your computer, and they pause until you're back. They're a handy extra when you're at your screen, not a set-and-forget safety net to leave running overnight. Your ordinary take-profit and stop-loss, set on the chart, stay in place either way.
</GuideCallout>

## So if the screen ever breaks mid-trade

That's the exact worry the backend answers. If TradingView goes down while you're in a trade, you're not stuck watching it. Log in to your backend, find the position, and close or adjust it there. It's the same trade, reached a different way, and that's the whole point of knowing where it is before you ever need it.
