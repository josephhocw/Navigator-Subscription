These are the skills that make the setups work. Before you trade a single setup, learn to judge whether a channel is even worth trading, draw the levels the Navigator leaves off, time your entries and exits, and read two timeframes at once. Get these right and the setups in the next stage become far more reliable.

## Pick the best channel

A good channel is one where price keeps bouncing cleanly off the lines — resistance holds, support holds, and the middle line gets respected. A messy channel, where price drifts straight through the lines without reacting, isn't worth trading.

The trick is to compare timeframes. The same market on a 1-hour, 2-hour and 3-hour chart can give very different channels — switch between them and pick the one where the lines behave best. The Navigator also draws a channel in green when it's capturing the trend well, which is a quick first check.

## Draw your own parallel lines

**Why bother?** Lines you draw by hand surface extra support and resistance levels the Navigator doesn't plot, so you know where to take profit earlier. The Navigator could draw more lines itself, but it's deliberately limited to keep the chart clean.

**When to draw one:**

- *Price overshoots the Navigator's lines.* Draw a parallel line at the level of the overshoot.
- *The channel is wide.* It's unrealistic to expect price to travel all the way from the middle to support or resistance, so a hand-drawn line gives you a nearer, more realistic target.

<GuideShot src="/learn/lessons/overshoot-hand-drawn-line.png" caption="Price overshot the channel, so a hand-drawn line marks the new level to watch." />

<GuideShot src="/learn/lessons/wide-channel-hand-drawn-line.png" caption="A wide channel: the hand-drawn lines give nearer, more realistic targets than the far side of the channel." />

**How to do it:** use TradingView's trendline tool. Start your line on the support or resistance line, then rotate it until it runs parallel.

<GuideShot src="/learn/lessons/trendline-tool.png" caption="The trendline tool lives in the left toolbar." />

Then right-click your new line and choose **Clone**, and move the copy until it connects with other turning points on the chart. A valid line should touch at least 2 to 3 turning points — the more it connects, the stronger it is as support or resistance.

<GuideShot src="/learn/lessons/line-through-pivots.png" caption="This line touches three turning points, so it carries real weight as resistance." />

## Enter and exit at the right time

There are two techniques here.

### Secondary trendlines inside the channel

Inside the Navigator's main channel there are usually smaller trends running their own course. You don't have to wait for a buy or sell label — you can act earlier off these secondary trends.

<GuideShot src="/learn/lessons/secondary-trends.png" caption="Smaller trends (pink) running their own course inside the main channel (blue)." />

Draw a secondary trendline by connecting the swing points (the spots where price turned) with a best-fit line.

<GuideShot src="/learn/lessons/swing-point-trendline.png" caption="Connect the swing points with a best-fit line." />

**Buy** when price breaks out above a secondary downtrend line — the breakout bar is your cue.

<GuideShot src="/learn/lessons/secondary-breakout-buy.png" caption="The breakout bar closes above the secondary downtrend line. That's the buy cue." />

**Sell** when price breaks down below a secondary uptrend line.

<GuideShot src="/learn/lessons/secondary-breakdown-sell.png" caption="Price breaks below the secondary uptrend line. That's the sell cue." />

When price reaches the main channel's support or resistance, enter or exit as the secondary channel breaks.

<GuideShot src="/learn/lessons/secondary-break-at-support.png" caption="Price reached the main channel's resistance and the secondary trend broke: sell at the break, take profit lower down." />

After buying on a breakout, you don't have to take profit until price breaks below an uptrend line. Keep an eye on the nearest support, especially during an uptrend.

### The two-bar theory

A simple price-action confirmation:

- For a sell entry or exit: act when price closes **below the last two bars** before it.
- For a buy: act when price closes **above the last two bars** before it.

<GuideShot src="/learn/lessons/two-bar-theory.png" caption="A sell off the two-bar rule: price closes below the two bars marked 1st and 2nd, and the TP arrow shows the exit." />

In a strong downtrend you may struggle to find a bar that closes above the last two — and that difficulty is itself confirmation of how strong the trend is. Place your stop above the resistance, or act when price breaks above the downtrend channel.

## Trade with two timeframes

**The trouble with one timeframe.** Watch only a higher timeframe and you'll see the trend clearly but your entries will be poor — too early or too late, without enough detail. Watch only a lower timeframe and you'll see every wiggle but miss the bigger picture, and end up taking trades against the larger trend.

**The method:**

- The **higher timeframe gives you direction.** Ask one question: should I be a buyer or a seller right now? Read the Navigator's trend and whether price is at support or resistance, then trade with that bias. Don't argue with the higher chart.
- The **lower timeframe gives you precision.** Zoom in for a cleaner entry in the same direction.

<GuideShot src="/learn/lessons/higher-timeframe.png" caption="The higher timeframe: a 15-minute chart. The two vertical lines mark the window shown below." />

<GuideShot src="/learn/lessons/lower-timeframe.png" caption="The same two and a half hours on a 30-second chart: the detail you need for a precise entry." />

Putting it together: get your bias from the higher chart, wait for a clean signal in that direction on the lower chart, then enter on the lower chart, set your stop, and manage the trade.

### Pairing the two timeframes

Keep the lower timeframe roughly 10 to 25 times smaller than the higher. Any closer and the two charts show you the same thing; any wider and they lose their connection. Three pairings that work:

- **Daily with 1-hour** (about 24 times) — for swing traders holding positions for days to weeks.
- **1-hour with 5-minute** (about 20 times) — for trading within the day.
- **15-minute with 1-minute or 30-second** (15 to 30 times) — for very quick in-and-out trading.
