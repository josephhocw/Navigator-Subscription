This is the heart of trading the Navigator well. Four skills, and they build on each other: find a channel worth trading, draw the levels the Navigator leaves off, use those lines to enter and exit, and read two timeframes at once. Get these four right and the rest (the signals, the setups) gets a lot easier.

## Skill 1 — Pick the best channel

Nothing else works without this. The lines and the signals are only as good as the channel they're drawn on, so the first job on any chart is to find a channel worth trading.

### What a good channel looks like

A good channel is one where the lines are doing their job:

- **Price bounces off the lines.** It touches resistance and turns down, touches support and turns up, over and over.
- **The middle line gets respected.** Price pauses or turns at it on the way across the channel.
- **Very few bars poke through.** Price lives inside the channel instead of cutting through the lines.
- **The channel is green.** The Navigator draws the channel in green when it's capturing the trend well. A quick first check, not the whole answer.

<GuideShot src="/learn/lessons/channel-good-example.png" caption="A channel worth trading. Every circle marks price touching a line and turning: the lines are acting as real support and resistance." />

A bad channel is the opposite: price drifts straight through the lines without reacting, and the labels print where nothing happens. Any signal from a channel like that is unreliable. Don't trade it — find a better one.

### Try the timeframes that match how you trade

The Navigator draws a channel on every timeframe, but on any given market only some of them are worth trading. So step through the timeframes that suit your style, and keep the one where the lines behave best.

- **Trading in and out within minutes?** Try **1, 2, 3, 4 and 5 minutes**.
- **Holding for days or weeks?** Try **30 minutes, 1 hour, 2, 3 and 4 hours**.

On each timeframe, ask the same question: is price bouncing off the lines? If yes, you've found your channel. If not, switch to the next timeframe and look again. And if no timeframe gives you a clean channel, that's an answer too — trade a different market, or come back later.

## Skill 2 — Draw your own parallel lines

Once you trust the channel, its slope is reusable. Clone it into parallel lines and project them above or below, and you surface extra support and resistance levels the Navigator doesn't plot — levels that help you enter and exit earlier. The Navigator could draw more lines itself, but it's deliberately limited to keep the chart clean.

There are two scenarios where you should draw parallel lines.

### When price overshoots the Navigator's lines

When price pushes past the channel, draw a parallel line at the overshoot to mark the new level to watch — price tends to respect it on the next visit.

## Skill 3 — Enter and exit off the lines

On a good channel with your lines drawn, the lines do the work. They tell you where to get in, where to take profit, and where to put your stop.

### The entry — two steps

**Step 1: wait for price to reach a line.** A Navigator line (support or resistance), or one of your own parallel lines. No line touched, no trade.

<GuideCallout title="Pro tip: follow the trend">

The higher-probability trade is the one that goes with the trend. In an uptrend, wait for price to reach support and buy; in a downtrend, wait for price to reach resistance and sell.

</GuideCallout>

**Step 2: choose how you get in.** Two ways, and it's a trade-off between reward and safety.

  con="Nothing has confirmed the line will hold yet." />

<GuideOption tone="safe" pill="Safer" title="Wait for the rebound (Way 2)"

To time that rebound, work down this list and use the first method that's available to you:

1. **A secondary-trendline break, if there's a trend to break — the best option.**
2. **The two-bar theory — when there's no secondary trendline to draw.**
3. **Our signal label — the fallback.**

The first two get you in **earlier** than the signal label, which is why they come first.

#### 1. Secondary-trendline break

Inside the Navigator's main channel there are usually smaller trends running their own course. Connect the swing points (the spots where price turned) with a best-fit line, and you've got a secondary trendline.

When price reaches the main channel's support or resistance, watch that secondary trendline. **Buy** when price breaks out above a secondary downtrend line; **sell** when it breaks down below a secondary uptrend line. The break is your entry, earlier and at a better price than waiting for the label.

#### 2. The two-bar theory

If there's no clean secondary trendline to draw, fall back on this simple price-action check:

<GuideMediaCard src="/learn/lessons/two-bar-theory-buy.png" alt="A buy off the two-bar rule: a bar closes above the previous two bars' high">

For a **buy**, enter when a bar closes **above the last two bars** before it.

Here, price closes above the previous two bars' high — a buy.

</GuideMediaCard>

<GuideMediaCard src="/learn/lessons/two-bar-theory.png" alt="A sell off the two-bar rule: a bar closes below the previous two bars' low">

For a **sell**, enter when a bar closes **below the last two bars** before it.

Here, price closes below the previous two bars' low — a sell.

</GuideMediaCard>

In a strong trend you may struggle to find a bar that closes against it — and that difficulty is itself a sign of how strong the trend is.

#### 3. Our signal — the fallback

If neither of the above is available, the Navigator's own **B** or **S** signal is your cue. It prints once price has travelled 50% of the width back off the line — the same level the entry lines mark. It's the latest of the three entries, but it's automatic and it needs no drawing.

<GuideShot src="/learn/lessons/signal-fallback.png" caption="The B and S labels print automatically — the fallback entries." />

### Take profit — read it off the lines

Your take-profit targets are the lines themselves: the middle line first, then the far side of the channel, the extended lines, and any parallel lines you drew in skill 2. Scale out: close part of the position at the first line, then hold the rest to the next one. The closer a realistic line sits, the more sense it makes to bank profit there rather than hold out for the far side, especially in a wide channel.

### The stop loss — about one bar beyond the line

Put your stop roughly one average bar's height beyond the line you entered against: above resistance for a sell, below support for a buy. There's no fixed number of points or percentage — size it to how tall the bars on your chart actually are, so a normal wobble doesn't stop you out but a genuine break does.

## Skill 4 — Trade with two timeframes

**The trouble with one timeframe.** Watch only a higher timeframe and you'll see the trend clearly but your entries will be poor — too early or too late, without enough detail. Watch only a lower timeframe and you'll see every wiggle but miss the bigger picture, and end up taking trades against the larger trend.

**The method:**

- The **higher timeframe gives you direction.** Ask one question: should I be a buyer or a seller right now? Read the Navigator's trend and whether price is at support or resistance, then trade with that bias. Don't argue with the higher chart.
- The **lower timeframe gives you precision.** Zoom in for a cleaner entry in the same direction, using the entry methods from skill 3.

<GuideShot src="/learn/lessons/higher-timeframe.png" caption="The higher timeframe: a 15-minute chart. The two vertical lines mark the window shown below." />

<GuideShot src="/learn/lessons/lower-timeframe.png" caption="The same two and a half hours on a 30-second chart: the detail you need for a precise entry." />

Putting it together: get your bias from the higher chart, wait for a clean signal in that direction on the lower chart, then enter on the lower chart, set your stop, and manage the trade.

### Pairing the two timeframes

Keep the lower timeframe roughly 10 to 25 times smaller than the higher. Any closer and the two charts show you the same thing; any wider and they lose their connection. Three pairings that work:

- **Daily with 1-hour** (about 24 times) — for swing traders holding positions for days to weeks.
- **1-hour with 5-minute** (about 20 times) — for trading within the day.
- **15-minute with 1-minute or 30-second** (15 to 30 times) — for very quick in-and-out trading.
