This is your reference manual for the chart. Once the Navigator is attached, the screen fills with lines, labels and panels. This page explains what each one means. When you're ready to put them to work, move on to the [core skills](/guides/master/core-skills).

## What you see on the chart

With the Navigator attached, your chart shows:

- **The current channel** — a sloped price channel made of a resistance line on top, a support line at the bottom, and a middle line halfway between them.
- **The previous channel** — the channel that was in force before the latest one formed, shown faded. Handy for reference once a new channel starts.
- **Extended lines** — an extended resistance line above the channel and an extended support line below it. These mark the next levels if price overshoots the channel.
- **The MA 5** — one moving average, the orange line, that follows the last five bars. It is on by default and its length can be changed in the settings.
- **Two information panels** — Trend Analysis at the top and Channel Levels at the bottom. The Channel Levels panel starts hidden, and both can be toggled in the settings.
- **Labels** — letters printed on the bars as things happen: support and resistance hits, buy and sell signals, breakouts, and markers showing where a new channel began.

## The channel lines

- **Resistance** — the top of the channel. When price reaches it, price is expected to fall.
- **Support** — the bottom of the channel. When price reaches it, price is expected to rise.
- **Middle** — halfway between support and resistance. This is the first take-profit reference in the core skills.
- **Extended resistance** — the next resistance above the channel, used when price overshoots the top.
- **Extended support** — the next support below the channel, used when price overshoots the bottom.

<GuideShot src="/learn/understanding/channel-lines.png" caption="The full set: resistance and support with the middle support/resistance line between them, the extended resistance above and the extended support below." />

### The green "good channel"

When the channel is drawn in green, the Navigator is telling you it's a good channel — one that's capturing the trend well. Think of it as a quick confidence check: green means the channel is reliable right now. Judging a channel properly is the first core skill, covered in the [next stage](/guides/master/core-skills).

### How a buy or sell signal forms

A hit on its own is not an entry. The Navigator waits for price to prove it is turning before it prints **B** or **S**. For a buy:

1. **Price hits support.** The S label marks it.
2. **Three bars in a row close above the orange MA 5.** Bars 2 and 3 also close above the support line. Bar 1 is allowed to close below the line, as long as it closed above the MA 5.
3. **One of those bars pushes past the bar before it** — bar 2 closes above bar 1's high, or bar 3 closes above bar 2's high. A real push, not a drift.
4. **On bar 3, the MA 5 is sloping up.**
5. **B prints on bar 3, at its close.** That close is the entry.

So a buy can form two ways: bar 1 closes below support but above the MA 5, then bars 2 and 3 close above both; or all three bars close above both the line and the MA 5. The first way gets you in one bar earlier.

The setup stays live for 30 bars after the hit. If price closes above the middle line first, the setup is dropped. One signal per hit.

<GuideShot src="/learn/understanding/signal-buy-steps.png" caption="A buy: three bars in a row (1, 2, 3) close above the orange MA 5, the last two above support as well, and B prints on bar 3 at its close." alt="Three numbered bars closing above the MA 5 after a support hit, with the B label on the third bar" />

**Extended buy (EB)** follows the same steps with three differences: the hit is the second touch of the extended support line (the ES2 label), bars 2 and 3 close above the extended support line, and bar 3 must close no higher than three-quarters of the way from extended support up to support. The first target of an EB is the support line, so at least a quarter of that distance has to be left for profit. If bar 3 closes past the three-quarter mark, no EB prints.

<GuideShot src="/learn/understanding/signal-extended-buy-steps.png" caption="An extended buy: the second extended-support hit (ES2), then three bars (1, 2, 3) close above the MA 5 and EB prints on bar 3." alt="Three numbered bars closing above the MA 5 after a second extended-support hit, with the EB label on the third bar" />

**Sells (S and ES) mirror everything downward:** a resistance hit, three bars closing below the MA 5 with bars 2 and 3 also below resistance (or extended resistance), one bar closing below the previous bar's low, the MA 5 sloping down on bar 3, and S or ES printing at bar 3's close. For an extended sell, bar 3 closes no lower than three-quarters of the way from extended resistance down to resistance.

<GuideCallout title="When your own judgement beats the signal">

The three-bar rule is there so the entry is confirmed, and confirmation costs bars. When the bars are big or the channel is narrow, price can reach the other side of the channel within two bars, before bar 3 has closed, and the opportunity is gone. The label is the safe default. If you can read the chart, you may enter earlier on the line hit when the channel is narrow compared with the bar size, with the same stop loss. The [core skills](/guides/master/core-skills) teach the earlier entries.

</GuideCallout>

<GuideShot src="/learn/understanding/signal-missed-narrow-channel.png" caption="A narrow channel and big bars: price hits resistance and is at support two bars later. By the time a signal could confirm, the move is over." alt="A narrow channel where price travels from the resistance hit to the support line within two large bars" />

### Looking back at older channels

To see an older channel and the signals that fired on it, use TradingView's **Bar Replay** tool in the top panel. Set the blue bar-selector to the bar just before the grey <span style="display:inline-block;background:#767676;color:#fff;font-weight:700;padding:0 8px;border-radius:6px;font-size:0.85em;line-height:1.6;">NC</span> label, and the chart redraws the previous channel with all its labels.

## The two panels

### Trend Analysis panel

This panel tells you two things about the current channel:

- **Trend direction** — uptrend, sideways, or downtrend.
- **Width** — how wide the channel is compared with the market's normal movement. It's expressed as a multiple of the Average True Range, so you might see "Wide (12.1x ATR)". The bigger the number, the wider the channel relative to how much price usually moves.

<GuideShot src="/learn/understanding/trend-analysis-panel.png" caption="This channel is a downtrend, and wide: 12.1 times the market's normal range." />

### Channel Levels panel

This one lists the exact prices of the current channel's levels on the latest bar: resistance (R), middle (M), support (S), extended resistance (ER) and extended support (ES). It starts hidden — turn it on under UI Panels in the settings when you want the numbers.

<GuideShot src="/learn/understanding/channel-levels-panel.png" caption="The Channel Levels panel: the current channel's exact levels." />

A practical use: if a new channel forms while you still hold an open position, the previous channel — still drawn faded on the chart — gives you your exit references.

### The SL lines

<GuideCallout title="The SL line is where your stop goes">

The SL line is exactly where the [core skills](/guides/master/core-skills) tell you to place your stop loss: 1.5 bar heights beyond the line you entered against. Switch it on when you have a trade on, and off again afterwards so the chart stays clean.

</GuideCallout>

Each toggle under Chart Lines draws an SL (stop-loss) line beyond each side of its channel.

Both are **off by default, and we recommend leaving them off until you need them** — with every line showing, the chart crowds up fast.

<GuideShot src="/learn/understanding/zone-toggles.png" caption="The two toggles under Chart Lines in the settings window. Both start unticked." />

One term before the cards: a **bar's height** is the distance from its **lowest point to its highest**. When a new channel forms, the Navigator measures the height of a **typical bar at that moment** and places each SL line **1.5 bar heights** beyond the level it protects.
