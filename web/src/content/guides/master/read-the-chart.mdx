This is your reference manual for the chart. Once the Navigator is attached, the screen fills with lines, labels and panels. This page explains what each one means. When you're ready to put them to work, move on to the [core skills](/guides/master/core-skills).

## What you see on the chart

With the Navigator attached, your chart shows:

- **The current channel** — a sloped price channel made of a resistance line on top, a support line at the bottom, and a middle line halfway between them.
- **The previous channel** — the channel that was in force before the latest one formed, shown faded. Handy for reference once a new channel starts.
- **Extended lines** — an extended resistance line above the channel and an extended support line below it. These mark the next levels if price overshoots the channel.
- **Two moving averages** — the 9 MA (faster) and the 30 MA (slower). The 30 MA starts hidden; turn it on in the settings if you want it.
- **Two information panels** — Trend Analysis at the top and Channel Levels at the bottom. The Channel Levels panel starts hidden, and both can be toggled in the settings.
- **Labels** — letters printed on the bars as things happen: support and resistance hits, buy and sell signals, breakouts, and markers showing where a new channel began.

## The channel lines

- **Resistance** — the top of the channel. When price reaches it, price is expected to fall.
- **Support** — the bottom of the channel. When price reaches it, price is expected to rise.
- **Middle** — halfway between support and resistance. This is the first take-profit reference in the core skills.
- **Extended resistance** — the next resistance above the channel, used when price overshoots the top.
- **Extended support** — the next support below the channel, used when price overshoots the bottom.

<GuideShot src="/learn/understanding/channel-lines.png" caption="The full set: resistance and support with the middle support/resistance line between them, the extended resistance above and the extended support below." />

### The green "good channel"

When the channel is drawn in green, the Navigator is telling you it's a good channel — one that's capturing the trend well. Think of it as a quick confidence check: green means the channel is reliable right now. Judging a channel properly is the first core skill, covered in the [next stage](/guides/master/core-skills).

## The two panels

### Trend Analysis panel

This panel tells you two things about the current channel:

- **Trend direction** — uptrend, sideways, or downtrend.
- **Width** — how wide the channel is compared with the market's normal movement. It's expressed as a multiple of the Average True Range, so you might see "Wide (12.1x ATR)". The bigger the number, the wider the channel relative to how much price usually moves.

<GuideShot src="/learn/understanding/trend-analysis-panel.png" caption="This channel is a downtrend, and wide: 12.1 times the market's normal range." />

### Channel Levels panel

This one lists the exact prices of the current channel's levels on the latest bar: resistance (R), middle (M), support (S), extended resistance (ER) and extended support (ES). It starts hidden — turn it on under UI Panels in the settings when you want the numbers.

<GuideShot src="/learn/understanding/channel-levels-panel.png" caption="The Channel Levels panel: the current channel's exact levels." />

A practical use: if a new channel forms while you still hold an open position, the previous channel — still drawn faded on the chart — gives you your exit references.

### The entry and SL lines

<GuideCallout title="These levels are a fall-back, not the method">

Treat the entry and SL lines as a guide for when you want a quick reference. The best way to enter and place your stop loss is taught in the next stage, the [core skills](/guides/master/core-skills).

</GuideCallout>

Each toggle under Chart Lines draws two things for its channel: the entry lines the buy and sell signals are built on, and an SL (stop-loss) line beyond each side.

Both are **off by default, and we recommend leaving them off until you need them** — with every line showing, the chart crowds up fast.

<GuideShot src="/learn/understanding/zone-toggles.png" caption="The two toggles under Chart Lines in the settings window. Both start unticked." />

One term before the cards: a **bar's height** is the distance from its **lowest point to its highest**. When a new channel forms, the Navigator measures the height of a **typical bar at that moment** and places each SL line **1.5 bar heights** beyond the level it protects.
