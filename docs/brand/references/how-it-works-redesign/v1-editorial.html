The channel

Three lines that show you where price turns

Once you can see the ceiling and the floor, a chart stops looking random. The Navigator draws a channel around price and keeps redrawing it as the market moves. Price tends to react at the three lines, and that is where it looks for a trade.

Resistance, the ceiling

Where price often stops rising and falls back.

The middle line

The halfway mark. It is the first place a trade aims for.

Support, the floor

Where price often stops falling and bounces.

Example one · buy the rebound

You wait for the bounce, then buy

Price falls to the support floor and reacts. The Navigator does not buy you in at support, where a falling market can still drop further. It waits.

When price has climbed back up about halfway to the middle line, the bounce is confirmed and the buy label prints. Your stop sits just below support, so the risk is small and known. The first target is the middle line, then the ceiling above it.

Example two · sell the pullback

The mirror image, near the ceiling

Price rises to the resistance ceiling and stalls. Again the Navigator does not sell you in right at the line. A strong market can push higher first.

It waits for price to pull back down about halfway to the middle line, and the sell label prints there. The stop sits just above resistance, and the first target is the middle line, then the floor below.

Example three · catch the breakout

When price breaks through and keeps going

Sometimes price does not turn at a level. It pushes through and runs. The Navigator marks this too, but it still waits for proof.

The breakout buy prints after a candle closes above resistance, once the ceiling has clearly given way. The mirror is a breakdown: a sell after price closes below support. Because the lines were drawn in advance, you catch the move early instead of chasing it.
