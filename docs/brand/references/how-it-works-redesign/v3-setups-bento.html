The three setups

Three ways the Navigator reads a chart

The Navigator never catches a falling or rising market at the line. It waits for the bounce, the pullback or the break to get going, then marks the entry, the protective stop and the first target. Each card below is one complete trade.

Setup 1 · Support

Buy the rebound

▲ Buy

Price falls to the support floor and reacts. The Navigator does not buy there. It waits until price has bounced back up about halfway to the middle line, and the buy label fires then, with the bounce already under way. Your stop sits just below support; your first target is the middle line, then resistance.

Entry — where the buy fires Stop — just below support Target — middle line, then resistance Middle line

Setup 2 · Resistance

Sell the pullback

▼ Sell

The mirror image. Price rises to the resistance ceiling and stalls. You wait for it to pull back about halfway to the middle line, and the sell label fires there. Your stop sits just above resistance; your first target is the middle line, then support.

Setup 3 · The break

Catch the breakout

▲ Breakout

Sometimes price pushes straight through a level and keeps going. You buy after a candle closes above resistance, not at the line itself. The mirror is a breakdown: you sell after price closes below support. Knowing the lines in advance is how you catch the move early instead of chasing it.

The channel

Three lines that show you where price turns

Once you can see the ceiling and the floor, a chart stops looking random. Price tends to react at these three lines, and the channel redraws itself as the market moves to keep up.

Resistance — the ceiling

Where price often stops rising and turns back down. The Navigator looks for sells here.

Middle line

The midpoint of the channel, and the first target for both a buy and a sell.

Support — the floor

Where price often stops falling and bounces. The Navigator looks for buys here.
