The chart, explained

Three lines, then the labels that do the watching for you

The Navigator draws a channel around price: a floor where price tends to bounce (support), a ceiling where it tends to stall (resistance), and a dashed middle line between them. The whole channel redraws itself as the market moves. The clever part is what it does next — it waits for price to react before it tells you anything.

The key idea: the Navigator never catches a falling knife. It doesn't buy the instant price hits the floor or sell the instant it hits the ceiling. It waits for the bounce, the pull-back or the break to start — that's the bit that makes it worth following. Read the three setups below in order.

▲Setup 1

Buy the rebound

1

Price drops to the floor. It falls all the way down to the support line and reacts there. Tempting to buy — but the Navigator stays quiet.

2

It bounces back up. Price lifts off support and climbs roughly halfway towards the middle line. Now the turn is real, not a guess.

3

The buy label fires here — after the bounce is under way, about halfway up. That's your signal to consider a buy.

4

Stop and target are set. Your stop sits just below the floor. The first target is the middle line, then the ceiling above it.

Why wait for the bounce? Because a level can be tested and still break. Waiting for the turn is what keeps you out of the falling knife.
Support & resistance Middle line Up candle (closed higher) Down candle (closed lower)
▼Setup 2

Sell the pull-back

1

Price rises to the ceiling. It runs all the way up to the resistance line and stalls. Again, the Navigator holds off.

2

It pulls back down. Price slips off resistance and falls roughly halfway towards the middle line. The roll-over is confirmed.

3

The sell label fires here — after the pull-back has started, about halfway down. That's your cue to consider a sell.

4

Stop and target are set. Your stop sits just above the ceiling. The first target is the middle line, then the floor below.

It's the same logic, flipped. Up at the ceiling you wait for the roll-over; down at the floor you wait for the bounce.
▲Setup 3

Catch the breakout

1

Price pushes into the ceiling. Instead of stalling, it presses right up against resistance and keeps trying.

2

A candle closes above it. One strong candle finishes clearly above the resistance line. That close is the breakout — the level has given way.

3

The breakout label fires here — after the close above resistance, not at the line. You join a move that's already proven itself.

The mirror is a breakdown: a candle closes below support, and a sell fires the same way. Knowing the lines in advance is how you catch the break early instead of chasing it.
